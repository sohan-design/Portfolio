Project context & business goals

Edmingle is an online teaching platform that helps institutions and educators deliver courses at scale. As the product evolved — especially with new AI-powered features — the website became a bottleneck.

The core business goal was clear: redesign edmingle.com to stay competitive with the product’s new feature set, with the AI page as the centrepiece of the revamp.

The product had moved forward. The website needed to catch up — and lead with AI.
***********

Stakeholder alignment & brief

The founders were fully aligned on the need for a revamp. They understood that the site wasn’t reflecting where the product was headed — and that enterprise buyers were the audience that mattered most going forward.

They gave us a one-month window to deliver. That tight timeline shaped every decision: ship the hero first, iterate on core pages, and save deeper content pillars for phase two.

  1. Reposition Edmingle from creator tool to enterprise-ready platform
  2. Make AI the headline feature across the site
  3. Ship fast — MVP hero in week one, full revamp by month end
***********

Audience research & competitive analysis

Before touching a single frame, we looked at what competitors were doing — and the pattern was unmistakable.

Edtech players who had started with colourful, creator-focused marketing sites were replacing them with clean, org-focused experiences. Less noise, clearer value props, and messaging built for decision-makers rather than solo educators.

What we learned

  1. Enterprise buyers expect simplicity and credibility — not playful gradients and feature dumps
  2. AI capabilities need dedicated, prominent real estate — not buried in a features dropdown
  3. Competitors were winning on clarity of message, not visual complexity
***********

Content strategy & information architecture

Content was handled directly by the founders — they knew the product and the market better than anyone. Together, we structured the site around a clear narrative.

The centrepiece of the content strategy was the Four Pillars of Teaching Online — a framework that mapped Edmingle’s capabilities to the real workflows institutions care about:

  1. Create — build and structure courses
  2. Deliver — run live and async sessions at scale
  3. Engage — keep learners active and accountable
  4. Grow — measure outcomes and expand reach

This framework gave us a scalable IA: every product feature could slot into a pillar, making the site easier to navigate and easier to update as new capabilities shipped.

***********

Key page designs

With a one-month timeline, we phased the work deliberately — highest-impact pages first, deeper content second.

Phase 1 — Hero & core pages

The hero shipped as an MVP in the first week. It established the new visual language — cleaner typography, org-focused messaging, and AI as the lead story — so we could validate direction before committing to the full site.

Once the hero was approved, we rolled the new styles across the main pages: Features, Pricing, and the dedicated AI page.

Phase 2 — Four Pillars pages

The Four Pillars content pages were scoped for phase two — deeper storytelling for each pillar, with case studies and use-case walkthroughs. The IA was built in phase one so these pages could slot in without restructuring the site.

***********

Conversion & UX considerations

Beyond the main site, we also built dedicated landing pages for paid ad campaigns — each focused on a single conversion goal with minimal navigation and a clear CTA.

For performance, we chose the Astra theme on WordPress. It gave us the simplicity and speed we needed without sacrificing layout flexibility — critical when page load score directly affects ad quality and organic ranking.

  1. Lightweight theme for strong Core Web Vitals scores
  2. Separate landing pages per campaign — no shared nav to leak traffic
  3. AI page optimised as the primary enterprise conversion path
***********

Final designs & handoff

Design and development ran in parallel throughout the month — there was no room for a traditional waterfall handoff.

We used Figma for high-fidelity designs and Framer for interactive prototypes, then converted approved frames directly to code. This kept feedback loops tight and let dev start building pages while design was still iterating on others.

***********

Performance results

Within weeks of the new site going live, the shift in audience was measurable.

  1. Enterprise AI demand: We saw a 35% increase in enterprise consumers who specifically reached out demanding AI features — validating that leading with AI on the website was the right call.
  2. Positioning shift: Inbound conversations moved from ‘can I use this for my course?’ to ‘how does your AI work for our institution?’
  3. Speed to ship: The one-month timeline held — hero in week one, core pages live by week three, ad landing pages running alongside.
The website finally told the same story the product did — and enterprise buyers noticed.
