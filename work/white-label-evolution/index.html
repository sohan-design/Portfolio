Project context

The Learner App is a learner-side application available on Web, Android, and iOS. It is a fully white-labelled product owned by the client. In this LMS, tutors upload course materials through the admin panel. The learner-side web and mobile apps are how learners access and consume that content.

Interface problems

  • Monotony in the interface — little colour variation
  • Outdated visual style
  • Non-uniform experience from the absence of a design system
  • Flat UI with minimal depth and almost no shadow usage

Business problems

  • Tutors needed more revenue from a single lead, but the platform did not support upselling.
  • Clients wanted to sell more than courses. The LMS limited both its own growth and that of its clients by focusing only on course sales.
Learning had to stay primary — while marketing and monetization finally got real estate that didn’t feel cheap.
***********

Stakeholder alignment

Around 80% of clients demanded an upselling mechanism. Tutors wanted to generate more revenue from a single lead; the product only sold courses.

Leadership aligned on two parallel goals for the sprint:

  1. Give clients monetization tools beyond courses — banners, memberships, blogs, events, communities, and stronger referrals
  2. Modernize the interface so white-label brands felt premium, not dated

That brief set a hard constraint: marketing visibility could not come at the cost of the learning experience.

***********

Research

Problems were discovered through a mix of qualitative and quantitative signals:

  • User research and personal observations while learning on other LMS platforms
  • Competitive research across peer products
  • Support tickets and client calls
  • Learner behaviour and pain points via tools like PostHog and MS Clarity
  • Product intuition built through continuous usage and exposure

The pattern was consistent: the dashboard looked dated, felt flat, and offered no graceful path for promotions — so clients either avoided monetization or plastered ads in ways that hurt first impressions.

***********

Content & IA

Showing ads in the most visible area of a learner dashboard is not a designer’s dream. The first impression could easily look cheap if banners dominate the top section.

We structured the banner module so learning stays primary:

  1. The first card is always a Continue Learning card
  2. After a 2-second delay, promotional cards auto-scroll sequentially
  3. Marketing gains visibility without owning the first paint

Beyond banners, we expanded monetization with memberships, blogs, events, communities, and a stronger referral system — so clients could grow revenue without rebuilding the product around a single course catalog.

***********

Key page designs

Marketing banner system

Demand for upselling was high (~60% of the monetization ask). Visibility for the banner strip reached 100% of the learner home surface; adoption landed above 60% of clients.

Modernising the interface

  • White base with subtle shadows for depth
  • Controlled, WCAG-compliant colour palette
  • Reduced brand colour overuse
  • Improved contrast hierarchy
  • Natural-tone 3D illustrations for visual depth

Design system

We built a scalable system on Ant Design with defined rules, reusable components, layout and spacing standards, and documentation — so white-label themes stayed consistent across Web and mobile.

***********

Conversion & UX

The banner system was designed as a conversion surface that still respected learner intent.

  1. Learning-first first card — Continue Learning owns the initial impression
  2. Delayed promo scroll — marketing cards advance after 2 seconds so the dashboard never feels ad-heavy on load
  3. Accessible colour system — WCAG-compliant palette and clearer contrast hierarchy across white-label brands
Learning stays primary. Marketing gains visibility. The dashboard avoids feeling ad-heavy.
***********

Handoff

The design system was built for developer adoption — not just Figma polish.

  • Component rules and reusable patterns on Ant Design
  • Layout and spacing standards documented for Web and mobile
  • Handoff focused on ensuring engineers could theme white-label brands without inventing one-off UI

That reduced drift across client brands and made new monetization surfaces cheaper to ship later.

***********

Impact

The impact was significant — sales increased by 40%, and we acquired industry-leading enterprise clients within their domains, directly driving higher product revenue.

  1. 40% increase in sales after the learner experience and monetization redesign
  2. 10+ industry-leading clients won in their respective domains
  3. 60%+ adoption of the marketing banner system among clients who needed upselling
White-label stopped meaning “dated skin on a course player” — and started meaning a premium learning surface that could also sell.
