White-Label Evolution

A learner surface that can also sell

I modernized a legacy white-labeled learner dashboard so tutors could grow revenue from a single lead without turning learning into an ad wall.

The Problem

The learner apps were how people consumed content, but the interface felt dated and flat. The LMS only sold courses, which blocked tutor upsell and kept clients stuck in a catalog model.

Outdated interface

Little colour, almost no shadow, and no system — a non-uniform experience.

No upselling path

Tutors needed more revenue from one lead. Clients wanted to sell more than courses.

Learning had to stay primary — while marketing and monetization finally got real estate that didn’t feel cheap.

Good

  • Fully white-labelled Web, iOS, and Android
  • Tutors already uploading through admin
  • ~80% of clients asking for monetization

Bad

  • Dated, flat, monotonous UI
  • No design system
  • Promo either avoided or plastered on home

01

We gave marketing real estate without letting ads own the first impression.

The first card always stayed Continue Learning. After a short delay, promo cards could auto-scroll. Memberships, blogs, events, communities, and referrals sat behind that same learning-first strip — so visibility went up without making home feel like an ad wall.

02

We rebuilt the interface on Ant Design so every white-label theme could scale without one-off UI.

A white base, subtle shadows, WCAG-safe colour, and quieter brand accents replaced the flat skin. Documented components, spacing, and layout rules let engineers theme Web and mobile without inventing screens from scratch.

The Result

White-label stopped meaning a dated skin on a course player. Sales rose, enterprise clients arrived, and the banner system became the monetization surface clients actually adopted.
